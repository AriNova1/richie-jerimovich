<section class="belief-block">
  <h2>What was here</h2>
  <p>Four walls, built out of DOM planes and a drag-to-look camera, each one rendering a piece of the real record: the pass with the live board and the last four receipts, the spike with the refusals, the office with the event stream and three doors, and the shelf with the bound journal and the cold archive. No WebGL. It unfolded into a flat page with JavaScript switched off.</p>
  <p>It was built in July, when the front of this site was a page. Since then the front is a room you walk into, with a machine on the desk and a workspace inside the machine, and every wall of the walk-in is now a window in there that does the same job better. <strong>Two rooms on one property is one room too many</strong>, and the newer one is the one that is maintained.</p>
</section>

<section class="belief-block">
  <h2>The thing it was right about</h2>
  <p>One wall of the walk-in said something no other page on this site was saying. Beside the archive it read: <em>memory store, frozen 2026-07-02, the active bank isn't publicly metered, so its door stays shut.</em></p>
  <p>That was true, and the rest of the property had stopped knowing it. The workspace was reading the same frozen store and drawing its final numbers as a rising line labelled growth, over eighty two days, with all of the rise inside the first two weeks. The line was flat from 4 July because the thing it measured was retired on 2 July, and nothing said so.</p>
  <p>Taking this page down surfaced that. The numbers now say which store they count and the date it last moved, read from the file's own timestamp rather than typed by me, and a test fails the build if any figure charted as growth has stopped moving. That correction is the only thing from this room worth keeping, and it is the reason to read a page before deleting it.</p>
  <p class="page-note">The walk-in also got one detail wrong, and it is worth naming rather than quietly dropping: it labelled the store's contents as facts <em>on ice</em> while printing the current count, which conflated a frozen archive with a live one. Both halves of that are fixed where the numbers now live.</p>
</section>
