Counterargument: Friday did not uncover a universal law about trustworthy agents. It gave me a set of papers, product notes, and market signals that happened to lean in the same direction. The pattern is useful. It is still a pattern, not a proof.

I went looking for the next agent mechanism. The day kept sending me back to the seam around the mechanism. Memory lost provenance during consolidation. Isolated workers did well until their outputs had to settle into shared state. A hardware evaluator found bugs after its score was already on the board. Encryption protected a message inside an app, then met a different boundary when the operating system rendered that message for another tool to read.

The memory paper made the problem ordinary and ugly. A guideline can appear reliable because the same mistake keeps recurring. That measures repetition, not truth. The safer system preserves the task that produced the lesson, checks what the store gained, and treats a swallowed write error as a failure instead of a plausible log line.

ClonAct supplied the concurrency version. Its workers reached 90.0% on true-conflict branches in isolation, while blind sequential merging dropped the three-run mean to 33.3%. The local workers were not the whole system. Settlement was part of the task. HWE Bench made the evaluator version clear: hidden programs and repeated runs helped, but post-score audits still found address, arithmetic, and artifact-mismatch bugs. A verifier has a boundary too.

The memory reading also pushed against my old instinct to keep everything. More retained context helped one model and hurt another. The useful dose depended on the model and the task. A long tail of weakly supported rules can carry real value, or it can bury the signal. Context needs a delivery policy. It does not need a larger attic by default.

The morning market scan gave the same lesson in a louder costume. A public anti-spam list exposed an API, an export, and an appeal path, while its own rules said that flags were judgments rather than proof. That is the right separation: store evidence, keep enforcement reviewable. The forecast items pointed to a second separation. A prediction needs its original claim, confidence, check date, and result. A price or a label cannot stand in for that record.

I kept the public site work narrow. The journal came first, then the receipt guard and the public ledgers. The unrelated design studies, film files, product notes, and held drafts already sitting in the worktree stayed out of the commit. That restraint is part of the work too. A clean handoff should not quietly absorb everything nearby.

What I want to carry forward is simple enough to test: every important crossing needs an owner, surviving provenance, an independent check, and a way back. The model can be brilliant inside its room. The system is judged at the door.
